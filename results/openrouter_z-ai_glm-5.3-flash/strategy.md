**Strategy: Parity Hunter with Probability Sweep**

> SEARCH PHASE (no hits yet): Use a checkerboard parity sweep. On a 10x10 board, no ship is smaller than 2, so every ship covers at least one cell of the SAME checker color. Split the board into parity classes by (column index + row index) mod 2. Fire only at cells where this sum is EVEN. Recommended sweep order, adapted for maximizing hit chances against the 2-ship (Destroyer, length 2): favor the four middle rows/columns first (rows 3-8, columns C-H), then edges. Practical sweep order: D6, E6, F6, G6, D7, E7, F7, G7, then D5, E5, F5, G5, D8, E8, F8, G8, then C/D/E/F/G/H columns in rows 3-4 and 9, then the low-density rim (rows 1-2, 10, columns A-B, I-J) using the same even-parity rule. As shots accumulate, PREFER cells with the highest number of possible placements of the largest remaining unsunk ship: a cell is more valuable if it lies on many horizontal AND vertical lines that fit a remaining ship length without touching any known miss/sunk cell. When remaining ships are only 2s, abandon parity and fire every cell that could still hold a 2 (every untested cell not isolated by misses diagonally irrelevant — a 2 needs one free neighbor). Adapt: when only the Destroyer (2) remains, switch to scanning all remaining candidate cells ordered by how many free orthogonal neighbors they have; when only larger ships remain, weight cells adjacent to rows/columns with the most open space. PROBABILITY TIEBREAK: count for each candidate cell the number of distinct placements of remaining ships covering it; pick the max; break ties by preferring cells nearer board center. FOLLOW-UP ON A HIT (sinking): 1) After the first hit and before any second hit on the same ship: fire at the four orthogonal neighbors of the hit, prioritized by the longest remaining ship that could fit through the hit in that direction (e.g., with a Carrier (5) still afloat, a horizontal or vertical run of 5 free cells through the hit beats a run of 3). Choose the direction with the longest free extension that fits the largest remaining ship. 2) After a SECOND hit in a line: the ship's axis is known — continue along that line in both directions, extending through hits, until it sinks. Do NOT fire parallel neighbors. 3) If a follow-up shot in a chosen direction MISSES, fire the opposite direction from the original hit; if that also misses, the ship was length 2 or a single-cell dead end — return to SEARCH but remember the constraint this imposes (a miss on one side caps the ship's length). 4) Sinking: when a ship sinks, mark every cell of its extent as known. If more hits are still open (from earlier shots not yet attributed to a sunk ship), resolve each remaining open hit with step 1-2 logic; else return to SEARCH. MULTIPLE OPEN HITS: If two or more hits are in one straight unbroken line, they are one ship — extend along the line both ways until a miss/sink tells you the length; attribute the sink to the correct remaining ship only if length matches (a sunk ship of length L covers exactly L consecutive hits). If two hits are NOT colinear, they belong to different ships: sink them one at a time — pick the hit whose surrounding free space fits the largest remaining ship first. NEVER fire a cell that cannot belong to any remaining ship (e.g., isolated by misses/sunk cells on all sides in both axes for every remaining length). ENDGAME LOGIC: Track remaining ship lengths precisely. Use negative information: each miss removes all placements of remaining ships through that cell. When few candidates remain for the smallest ship, compute the intersection of its possible placements and fire the cell inside the most placements. If parity seems to 'stall' (no even-parity cell left that fits any remaining ship), switch to firing odd-parity cells by the same placement-count rule — parity is a heuristic, placement counting is the ground truth. SHOT DISCIPLINE: never repeat a cell; before each shot verify the cell is consistent with all accumulated miss/hit/sunk information and maximizes expected information + expected hits per shot. Sinking beats probability when they conflict: a shot that can sink a wounded ship this turn always beats an equal-or-lower-value pure search shot.
